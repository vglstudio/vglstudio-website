Both solutions run on the same premise: strategy governs the system, the system produces something measurable, and you own what gets built. No seats to fill, no platform to keep renting, and one place the number lands.

The problem

Most GTM stacks don't produce revenue

They produce activity, data and reports. Two distinct failures sit underneath that, and almost every engagement starts in one of them.

The pipeline problem

Too many disconnected tools

Nothing talks to anything else

CRM, data platform, email tool, social automation, analytics. The integration work never makes it to the top of anyone's list, so the seams stay manual.

Data platforms with no execution

ZoomInfo and Apollo hand you contacts

They hand you no orchestration, no sequencing and no outbound capability. The license renews whether or not anyone acted on it.

Automation without orchestration

Tools installed, not integrated

Manual handoffs everywhere, no single source of truth, and enrichment that happens only when someone remembers to run it.

SDR agencies that miss the ICP

High activity, low fit

Volume sprayed at a list nobody scored, with no strategic alignment to the positioning you actually sell on. Discovered around month four.

No senior GTM leadership

Junior operators managing tools

No revenue architect making the stack work as one system, and nobody accountable for the number at the end of it.

The build problem

The manual situation

Spreadsheets and email attachments

Work that runs on someone remembering to send the update. It functions, until it stops scaling, and nobody can see what is happening without asking.

The wrong-fit situation

A platform built for a different business

It half fits. Everyone works around it. The workarounds have quietly become the process, and the subscription renews anyway.

Why it stays that way

Building it never cleared the bar

It has historically meant a development team, a six-figure budget and two quarters. So the manual version survives another year.

Who this is for

Two seats we work from

Founder-led B2B companies

Seed through Series B and similar stages, in any B2B vertical. Growth runs through the founder, and every option for scaling past that has looked more expensive than the pipeline it would produce.

CROs and RevOps leaders

Mid-market and up, under pressure to show progress fast. The GTM stack has grown to six vendors producing activity, and there is no AI-native execution layer underneath it.

Activity without pipeline. Data without deals. And the system that would fix either one keeps losing the budget argument to the thing already running.

Why this is different

Built for outcomes, not tool adoption

The distinction that matters isn't which tools are in the stack. It's who is accountable for what comes out of it.

Managed service, not SaaS-only

We run the system and you get the results. There is no internal team to build first, and no seats sitting unused.

Governed by strategy, not volume

The engine is directed by a defined ICP and positioning, not by how many contacts a database can return. Strategy in is the whole premise.

Embedded GTM operators

Real revenue leadership is part of the engagement, not an upsell. Senior judgment on the account, not junior hands on the tools.

Mass market and ABM in parallel

Intent-led demand discovery and named-account research run at the same time, feeding the same orchestration layer.

Replaces multiple vendors

Data platform, outbound tooling, SDR retainer and the RevOps time spent connecting them, consolidated into one line item.

You own what we build

Data, contacts, CRM infrastructure and any system from the build lab. Nothing here is rented back to you.
