How it runs

From first signal to sales-ready conversation

  1. ICP and intent identification

    Accounts and decision-makers are scored against your ICP, surfacing who is showing buying signals, who is engaging on relevant topics, and which warm paths already run through your team's existing connections.

  2. AI-assisted outreach campaigns

    Multi-channel sequences across email and LinkedIn, written for executive buyers and sequenced for relevance rather than volume. Roughly 400 prioritized contacts a month, sent in small batches rather than blasts.

  3. Lead qualification and scoring

    Every contact carries an ICP-fit score with a documented reason, role, seniority, firmographics, engagement signal. Low-fit contacts are filtered out before they ever reach your team.

  4. Managed early-stage engagement

    The VGL team handles outreach, replies, data enrichment and RevOps oversight directly, so your leadership sees conversations worth having instead of raw lists to sort through.

  5. Pipeline delivered as qualified opportunities

    Opportunities sync into the VGL Studio CRM, or into yours, HubSpot, Salesforce, Pipedrive, Zoho and others. You own the data either way, during the engagement and after it.

Questions

AI managed pipeline, answered

What is AI managed pipeline?

AI managed pipeline is a single operated system that handles the entire top of the funnel: identifying high-fit accounts, scoring them against your ICP, running coordinated email and LinkedIn outreach, and qualifying the replies. Unlike a data platform or an outbound tool, it is delivered as a managed service, so nobody on your side runs it day to day.

The distinction that matters is accountability. A platform gives you access and leaves outcomes to you. A managed engine makes one party responsible for whether qualified conversations appear.

That changes what you are buying. Not seats, not contacts, not activity reports, a number of conversations with people who fit your ICP, produced by a system somebody else operates.

The VGL perspective

Most companies do not have a tooling problem. They have an ownership problem wearing a tooling costume.

How is managed pipeline different from an appointment setting service?

An appointment setter delivers meetings and nothing else. Managed pipeline delivers the ICP strategy that governs targeting, the orchestration layer connecting intelligence to execution, a CRM you own, and data that stays yours when the engagement ends. The difference shows up when you stop: an appointment setter leaves you where you started.

Appointment setting typically runs $6,000 to $10,000 a month and is measured in meetings booked. That metric is easy to hit and easy to hit badly, a meeting with a poor-fit account still counts.

Scoring every contact against a documented ICP reason, and filtering low-fit before it reaches your team, is what separates a conversation worth having from a calendar hold.

The VGL perspective

We price against appointment setting deliberately, because that is the honest comparison most buyers are actually making.

How long before managed pipeline produces qualified conversations?

Three to four weeks to the first qualified conversation. The first two weeks cover ICP definition, list building, domain and deliverability setup, and sequence configuration. Sends begin in week three at controlled volume. Compare that against three to six months to hire, onboard and ramp an internal team.

The setup time is not padding. Domain warmup in particular cannot be rushed without damaging inbox placement, and placement problems are expensive to undo.

Volume ramps deliberately from there. Small batches of fifty or fewer contacts consistently outperform large sends, so scaling is a function of signal quality rather than capacity.

The VGL perspective

The setup weeks are not padding. Everything that goes wrong in outbound goes wrong because somebody skipped them.

What does AI managed pipeline cost?

Engagements start between $2,500 and $5,000 per month depending on ICP complexity and team scope, with no long-term contract required. That compares against roughly $6,000 to $10,000 monthly for appointment setting, or about $12,000 monthly to build equivalent capability in-house across an SDR, a data license, outbound tooling and RevOps time.

The comparison that matters is scope per dollar rather than the headline number. An appointment setter at the same price delivers meetings. The engagement delivers strategy, orchestration, execution and infrastructure you keep.

Full line-item math for both comparisons is on the pricing page.

Do we own the data and contacts?

Yes. You own all data, contacts and CRM infrastructure throughout the engagement and after it ends. Opportunities sync into the VGL Studio CRM or directly into yours, including HubSpot, Salesforce, Pipedrive and Zoho. Nothing is rented back to you and nothing is withheld at exit.

Data ownership is where managed services quietly differ. Some agencies retain the contact database and the sequence history, which means leaving costs you the asset you paid to build.

Owning the infrastructure also means the engine keeps working if you bring it in-house later. That is a feature, not a risk we are hedging against.

The VGL perspective

If a partner cannot answer the data question in one sentence, that is the answer.

What is the difference between ICP data and intent data?

ICP data tells you who fits: industry, size, role, technology, geography. Intent data tells you who is moving: researching the category, engaging on relevant topics, or hitting a trigger event like a funding round or a new hire. Fit without timing wastes sequences. Timing without fit wastes your team's attention. The engine runs both together.

Most stacks buy one and improvise the other. A data platform sells fit. An intent vendor sells timing. Neither scores them together, so the prioritization work falls to whoever is running the list that week.

Trigger-based sends within 24 to 48 hours of an event, a new role, an expansion, a funding announcement, consistently outperform untimed outreach by a wide margin.

The VGL perspective

Fit and timing are the two halves of one decision, and almost nobody buys them as a single purchase.
