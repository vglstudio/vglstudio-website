Where is the growth we cannot see?

Hidden Growth & Sales Art

Where untapped growth actually hides, why companies stall, and how experienced operators decide what to do next.

What is hidden growth?

Hidden growth is opportunity a company already holds but cannot see. It sits in existing customers who bought once and never expanded, in data nobody turns into a decision, in an underused product, an adjacent market, a pricing structure, or a distribution channel nobody has built. It is rarely a new idea. It is almost always an existing asset that has never been deliberately worked, because everyone inside the business is fully occupied running what already works.

Why it matters

The reason it stays hidden is structural rather than intellectual. Nobody owns the question of what else is possible, so it never appears on a roadmap or in a forecast.

The second reason is that finding it requires crossing functional boundaries. The opportunity usually sits between two departments, which means it belongs to neither.

What to look at

  • Customers who bought once and never expanded
  • Data collected but never decided on
  • Markets adjacent to one you already serve
  • Channels nobody has deliberately built

The VGL perspective

This was the original VGL observation and it has held up across every engagement since. Most companies have more opportunity than they can see, and the constraint is attention rather than imagination.

How do you find untapped growth in a business?

Start where value is leaking rather than where you want growth to appear. Look at customers who bought once and did not expand, products with demand the company does not actively pursue, data that never becomes a decision, and partners already selling into your market. Then pick the single opportunity with the shortest path to evidence, and go get that evidence before building anything around it.

Why it matters

The discipline is choosing one. Most growth exercises produce a list of fifteen opportunities, which is functionally identical to producing none, because nothing gets the attention required to prove out.

Shortest path to evidence is the right selection criterion rather than largest theoretical value. A smaller opportunity you can test in six weeks teaches you more than a larger one you can only argue about.

What to look at

  • Where value leaks, not where growth is wanted
  • Shortest path to evidence as the tiebreaker
  • One opportunity, not a ranked list of fifteen

The VGL perspective

Win first, then scale. We would rather prove one thing in a quarter than plan five things for a year, and every engagement we have run confirms that preference.

What causes a company to stop growing?

Usually a constraint that moved rather than an effort that stopped. Growth running through founder relationships eventually hits the founder's capacity. Growth running through one channel hits that channel's ceiling. Growth running on undifferentiated positioning hits the point where the market has enough alternatives. The work is identifying which constraint is currently binding, because effort applied to any other one produces activity and no result.

Why it matters

This is why adding sellers so often fails to move the number. More sellers against a positioning constraint produces more conversations with people who were never going to buy.

It is also why the same intervention succeeds at one company and fails at another. The intervention was not the variable. The binding constraint was.

What to look at

  • Which constraint is binding right now
  • Whether the last intervention addressed it or bypassed it
  • What the constraint will become once this one clears

The VGL perspective

Knowing which lever matters now is the skill that is hardest to buy and hardest to systematise. It is what we mean by Sales Art, and it is the reason we embed rather than advise.

What is a growth operator?

A growth operator works across strategy, sales, marketing, product and partnerships rather than inside one of them, because the next binding constraint rarely respects functional boundaries. The defining skill is knowing what the business needs next, and in what order, rather than applying a fixed methodology regardless of situation. The difference from a consultant is that an operator executes what they recommend and stays long enough to be wrong in public.

Why it matters

A consultant is measured on the quality of the recommendation. An operator is measured on whether the number moved, which selects for very different behaviour.

It also changes what gets recommended. Advice you have to implement yourself tends to be more conservative and more specific than advice you hand over.

What to look at

  • Whether they execute or hand over
  • How long they stay after the recommendation
  • Whether the methodology adapts to the business

The VGL perspective

Sales Art is knowing when to think, when to act, and in what order. It is not a framework, which is exactly why it is hard to sell and worth buying.

How do partnerships actually generate revenue?

Through leverage rather than effort. One relationship that puts you in front of many customers at once, instead of one conversation producing one customer. Sell once, reach many. The partners worth building with are the ones whose economics improve materially when you succeed, because a partner who does not gain from selling you will not sell you twice regardless of what the agreement says.

Why it matters

This is the most under-designed part of most go-to-market plans. Companies build elaborate demand generation and then treat channel as a logo slide, when the channel is frequently the shorter path to the same buyers.

The design question is not who has access to our market. It is who makes more money when we win, because that is the only durable reason anyone sells on your behalf.

What to look at

  • Whether the partner's economics improve when you win
  • One relationship reaching many buyers, not one
  • Whether the partnership survives without a champion

The VGL perspective

Win as your partners win. Every channel programme we have seen fail was designed around what the vendor needed, and every one that worked was designed around what the partner earned.
